Getting Started in Mzansi Manager: Your First Season

Mzansi Manager is a free football management game where you take charge of a club, build a squad and try to climb from the lower divisions to the top. This guide walks you through your first ten minutes and your first season so you can start with confidence.

1. Choose your manager and your club

When you start a new career, you first pick a manager portrait and then choose a club. The game has 100 clubs spread across five divisions, from the Premier League at the top to the 5th Division at the bottom. Starting at the bottom is the hard road: smaller budgets, weaker squads and bigger challenges. Starting higher gives you better players, but the board expects more from you.

If this is your first game, a club in the middle divisions is a good balance. You have enough quality to win matches, and room to grow.

2. Check your squad before you play

Open the squad screen and look at the lineup. Each player has a rating and a position, such as goalkeeper, centre-back or striker. Look for three things: weak spots (positions where your best player is rated far below the rest), players out of position, and the balance between attack and defence. Fix obvious gaps first, because a missing striker or an injured goalkeeper costs more points than a small tactical mistake.

3. Know your board's objectives

Every season the board sets objectives for your club. These usually include a league target (for example, finishing in the top half), a cup target and a finance target (for example, not losing more than a set amount of money). Your progress against each one is shown on the board screen, along with your board confidence and job security. Meet your objectives and the board trusts you. Miss them badly and your job is at risk.

4. Play the matches

Use Continue to move to your next match. Before kick-off you see a preview of both teams and their lineups. You can play the match out, or use Quick sim to skip ahead when the fixture matters less. Watch the league table as the season progresses, because small gains in the table add up.

5. Handle transfers carefully

Transfers only happen while the window is open, and the game shows how many days remain until it opens or closes. You have a transfer budget and a wage budget, and your squad has a size limit and a limit on foreign players. Spend wisely: one good signing in a weak position is worth more than three average players. We cover this in detail in our transfer guide.

6. Watch your finances

Wages and transfer fees count against your income. A club that spends more than it earns will miss its finance objective and can get into trouble. Read more in the board and finances guide.

Quick tips for your first season

Save your career often. Do not sell your best players just for cash. Check injuries before big matches. Give young players a chance, because they improve over time. And be patient: promotion usually takes more than one season.
